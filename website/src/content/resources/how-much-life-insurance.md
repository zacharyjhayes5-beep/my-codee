Most people either skip life insurance entirely or pick a round number that
sounds like enough. There is a better way, and it does not take long.

## Start with the question underneath

Life insurance answers one question: **if your income stopped tomorrow, what
would the people who depend on you need in order to stay steady?**

Not "stay rich." Stay in the house, stay in the same school, and not have to
make enormous decisions in the middle of the worst year of their lives.

## A workable formula

Add these up:

1. **Income replacement.** Your annual take-home pay times the number of years
   your family would need it. Ten years is a reasonable starting point; use more
   if your kids are young.
2. **Debt.** The mortgage balance, plus anything co-signed or personally
   guaranteed.
3. **Future costs you want to fund.** College, a wedding, a business buyout.
4. **Final expenses.** Funeral and related costs — commonly $10,000 to $20,000.

Then subtract:

- Existing life insurance, including whatever you have through work
- Savings and investments genuinely available for this purpose

The number left over is your gap. That is what you are shopping for.

## Do not forget the non-earning parent

If one parent stays home, replacing that work — childcare, transportation,
household management — costs real money. Coverage on both parents is normal, not
excessive.

## Term is usually the right starting point

Term life covers a fixed stretch of years at a fixed price and delivers the most
coverage per dollar. It lines up naturally with the years when a mortgage and
kids overlap, which is exactly when the gap is biggest.

Permanent coverage has legitimate uses — final expenses, estate planning,
leaving something behind on purpose. But if someone leads with it before asking
about your mortgage and your kids' ages, be skeptical.

## The part nobody tells you

The price is based partly on your age and health **right now**, and it locks in
when the policy issues. A healthy 30-year-old will never see a better rate than
the one available today. Waiting has a real cost even if nothing goes wrong.
