A homeowners policy covers a lot. But the gaps tend to be specific, predictable,
and expensive — and most of them are fixable for a small amount of money if you
know they are there.

## 1. Flooding

Water that rises from the outside — an overflowing river, a saturated yard — is
not covered by a standard homeowners policy. Anywhere. Flood coverage is a
separate policy, through the federal program or a private carrier.

It is worth knowing your flood zone even if you are not in one. Zones get redrawn,
and a meaningful share of flood claims come from outside high-risk areas.

## 2. Water backing up through a drain or sump

Different from flooding, and equally excluded by default. A sump pump failing
during a spring thaw is a common West Michigan claim, and a finished basement
turns it into a large one.

This one is usually an inexpensive endorsement. If you have a finished basement,
add it.

## 3. The business you run from home

Business equipment and business liability at home are barely covered, or not at
all. If clients visit, if you store inventory, or if you own more than a laptop's
worth of gear, you need either an endorsement or a small commercial policy.

## 4. Anything valuable, past a low cap

Jewelry, firearms, tools, cameras, collectibles — these have built-in sublimits
that are lower than people expect, often a few thousand dollars for a whole
category. A single ring can exceed the entire jewelry limit.

The fix is to *schedule* the item: list it specifically, usually with an
appraisal. Scheduled items also typically skip the deductible.

## 5. Gradual damage and maintenance

Insurance covers sudden and accidental losses, not wear. A pipe that bursts is
covered; a pipe that has been seeping behind drywall for two years generally is
not. Same for roofs at the end of their life.

This is not a loophole so much as the shape of the product: it is for the
unexpected, not the inevitable.
