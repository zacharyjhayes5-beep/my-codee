Michigan used to be simple, in one respect at least: every auto policy came with
unlimited medical coverage after a crash. Since July 2019, you choose your level
instead. That choice is buried on one screen of most online quotes, and it is the
single most consequential decision on your policy.

## What PIP actually pays for

Personal Injury Protection covers **your own** injuries after a car accident,
regardless of who caused it. Not the other driver's — yours. It pays for:

- Hospital and rehabilitation care
- Long-term attendant care if injuries are severe
- Lost wages, up to a monthly maximum, for up to three years
- Replacement services — the things you would have done yourself, like mowing
  the lawn or driving the kids

That third and fourth item surprise people. PIP is not only a medical benefit;
it is an income and household benefit too.

## The levels

You are choosing a ceiling on the medical portion:

1. **Unlimited** — the old default. Costs the most, caps nothing.
2. **$500,000**
3. **$250,000**
4. **$250,000 with exclusions** — available if you have qualifying health coverage
5. **$50,000** — only for drivers enrolled in Medicaid
6. **Opt out entirely** — only with Medicare Parts A and B

## How to actually choose

The honest framing is this: a serious brain or spinal injury blows through
$250,000 quickly, and often within the first year. The lower levels are not a
bet on whether you are a good driver. They are a bet on what happens if the
worst version of a crash happens to you.

The right answer depends on your **health insurance**, and specifically on
whether your plan covers auto accident injuries and what its own limits look
like. A plan with a low out-of-pocket max and no auto exclusion is a genuinely
different situation than a high-deductible plan.

> Bring your health plan's summary of benefits to the conversation. This is the
> one document that changes the recommendation.

## The mistake I see most

Someone shops online, sees that dropping to $50,000 saves a meaningful amount
per month, and takes it — without noticing that the low tiers require Medicaid
enrollment to even be valid. Choosing a level you do not qualify for is a
problem you find out about at the worst possible time.

If you are not sure what you picked, pull out your declarations page or send it
over. It takes about five minutes to confirm, and it is worth doing.
