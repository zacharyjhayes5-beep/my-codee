Your declarations page — the "dec page" — is the summary at the front of your
policy. It is one or two pages, and almost nobody reads it. Here is how to get
what you need out of it quickly.

## Find these four things first

**1. Your liability limits.** Often written as something like `100/300/100`. That
means $100,000 per injured person, $300,000 per accident, and $100,000 for
property damage. This is the coverage protecting your savings if you cause
serious harm. It is also the number most commonly set too low, because it is
invisible until you need it.

**2. Your deductibles.** One for collision, one for comprehensive, one for the
home. Ask yourself honestly: could you write that check tomorrow? If yes, a
higher deductible is usually a good trade. If not, lower it.

**3. Your dwelling amount, on a home policy.** This should reflect what it would
cost to *rebuild*, not what you paid or what the house would sell for. If you
have renovated or finished a basement since the policy was written, this number
is probably stale.

**4. The endorsements list.** Usually near the bottom, in small type. This is
where water backup, service line, scheduled jewelry, and similar add-ons appear.
If you think you have a coverage and it is not listed here, you do not have it.

## Then check the boring stuff

- Are all drivers in the household listed?
- Are the vehicles and their usage right — commuting versus business?
- Is the mortgage company correct, if you refinanced?
- Do the dates line up with when you expect to renew?

## What to do with what you find

Circle anything that looks wrong or that you cannot explain, and ask. A good
agent should be able to walk through every line with you without getting
defensive about it.
